What is transcript-based video editing?

Traditional editing means scrubbing a timeline, listening for mistakes, and razor-cutting clips by hand. Transcript-based (or text-based) editing transcribes your recording first, so the video behaves like a document: delete a sentence in the text and it's cut from the video. For talking-head content — YouTube videos, podcasts, courses, UGC ads — it's dramatically faster, because the mistakes live in the words, not in the pixels.

Frequently asked questions

What is a transcript-based video editor?

A transcript-based (or text-based) video editor transcribes your footage and lets you edit the video by working with the words instead of a timeline. Cutting a sentence from the transcript cuts it from the video.

What footage works best with Easecut?

Talking-head and podcast-style recordings — single or multi-speaker footage where you talk to camera. That's what the AI detection is tuned for.

Do I still need a timeline editor?

Not for cutting. For motion graphics or color work you can export a tightened cut or an edit list into your NLE of choice and continue there.
