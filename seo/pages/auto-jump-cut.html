Auto jump cut editor for talking-head video

Jump cuts are the rhythm of modern creator video — and making them by hand is death by a thousand razor cuts. Easecut turns your raw recording into a jump-cut edit automatically: it finds the silences, retakes, and filler between your good moments, cuts them to the pacing you choose, and hands you the full list for a one-glance review before export.

Jump cuts without a timeline

Traditional jump cutting means scrubbing the waveform for every pause and mistake, then razoring both ends of each one. For a ten-minute raw take, that's easily a hundred manual cuts. Easecut replaces the whole process: voice-activity detection finds the gaps, transcript analysis finds the retakes and filler, and every cut is queued with its exact transcript line so you can audit it in seconds.

Pacing that fits your style

The difference between a relaxed vlog and a rapid-fire short is how hard you cut the gaps. Easecut ships pacing presets across that spectrum — from a natural rhythm that keeps conversational pauses breathing, to tight, aggressive cutting where dead air simply doesn't exist. And because cut edges are refined against where your voice actually stops, sentence endings stay intact instead of getting clipped mid-word.

Frequently asked questions

What is a jump cut?

A jump cut removes a slice of time from a continuous shot — the pause, the flub, the breath — so the video jumps straight to the next thing worth watching. It's the defining rhythm of talking-head YouTube and short-form content.

How does auto jump cutting work in Easecut?

Easecut analyzes both the audio and the transcript of your footage, finds the silences, retakes, and filler between the good moments, and turns each into a suggested jump cut. You pick the pacing, approve the cuts, and export.

Can I choose how aggressive the jump cuts are?

Yes — pacing presets range from a natural conversational rhythm to fast, tight cutting for short-form. A manual mode exposes every threshold if you want full control.

Do I need editing experience?

No. There's no timeline to learn — the workflow is upload, review a list of suggested cuts with their transcript lines, approve, export.
