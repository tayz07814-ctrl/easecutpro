A focused Descript alternative for cutting raw creator footage

Descript turned "edit video like a doc" into a whole studio — recording, transcription, AI voices, publishing. If what you actually need is the cutting part done for you, Easecut is a transcript-based video editor built around one job: turning raw talking-head footage into a tight, postable video with AI auto-cut.

Why creators look for a Descript alternative

Text-based editing is a genuine breakthrough: deleting a sentence in the transcript is faster than scrubbing a timeline. But with a full studio suite, the cutting itself is still mostly manual — you read the transcript, find the retakes and dead air yourself, and delete them line by line. For a 40-minute raw recording, that's still a long session.

Easecut inverts that workflow. Instead of handing you a transcript and a delete key, it reads the footage first and flags what should probably go: retakes, false starts, filler, production chatter ("hold on, let me fix my light"), repeated lines, and long pauses. You review a list of suggestions, approve or reject each one, and export.

Frequently asked questions

Is Easecut a Descript alternative?

Yes, for the editing part of the workflow. Like Descript, Easecut is transcript-based — you work with your video as text. Easecut focuses specifically on automatically cutting raw talking-head footage: retakes, false starts, long pauses, and production chatter.

Does Easecut edit my video automatically?

It suggests every cut automatically, but nothing is applied until you review and approve. You stay the final call on every edit.

Can I keep using my current editor?

Yes. Export a tightened cut or an edit list you can bring into the NLE of your choice, such as Premiere Pro, DaVinci Resolve, or Final Cut.
